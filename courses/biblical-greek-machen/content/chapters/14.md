# Unit 14 — The First Aorist

The first aorist active and middle indicative, and the constructions with πιστεύω.

## Summary

The aorist states a past action simply, without saying that it went on: ἔλυσα, I loosed. The imperfect (ἔλυον, I was loosing) is for continuing or repeated past action. The first aorist adds σα to the stem, with the augment and the secondary endings; the consonant changes before σ are the same as in the future (ἔπεμψα, ἐδίωξα, ἔπεισα).

## ἔλυσα, I loosed

| Person | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | ἔλυσα | ἐλύσαμεν |
| 2nd | ἔλυσας | ἐλύσατε |
| 3rd | ἔλυσε(ν) | ἔλυσαν |

## ἐλυσάμην, I loosed for myself

| Person | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | ἐλυσάμην | ἐλυσάμεθα |
| 2nd | ἐλύσω | ἐλύσασθε |
| 3rd | ἐλύσατο | ἐλύσαντο |

ἐλύσω comes from ἐλύσασο with the σ dropped. A deponent verb has a deponent aorist: δέχομαι, ἐδεξάμην.

## πιστεύω

πιστεύω takes the dative: πιστεύω τῷ ἀνθρώπῳ, I believe the man. With εἰς and the accusative it means believe in or on: πιστεύω εἰς τὸν κύριον.

## Common confusion

ἐλύσω (you loosed for yourself, aorist middle) and λύσω (I shall loose) differ only by the augment. The English perfect and pluperfect ("has prepared", "had healed") are often best given by the Greek aorist.
