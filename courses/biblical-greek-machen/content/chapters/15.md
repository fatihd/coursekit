# Unit 15 — The Second Aorist

The second aorist active and middle indicative.

## Summary

Some verbs make their aorist without σ, on a stem different from the present: λείπω, ἔλιπον. The second aorist has the endings of the imperfect, but on the aorist stem; ἔλειπον (I was leaving) is imperfect, ἔλιπον (I left) is aorist. Whether a verb has a first or second aorist, and what its aorist stem is, must be learned with the verb. Several common verbs use an aorist from a different root: λέγω, εἶπον; βλέπω (ὁράω), εἶδον; ἔρχομαι, ἦλθον; φέρω, ἤνεγκα.

## The aorists of this unit

ἔβαλον (βάλλω), ἐγενόμην (γίνομαι), εἶδον (βλέπω), εἶπον (λέγω), ἔλαβον (λαμβάνω), ἤγαγον (ἄγω), ἦλθον (ἔρχομαι), ἔπεσον (πίπτω), and the first aorist ἤνεγκα (φέρω), with κα instead of σα. ὄψομαι is the future of βλέπω.

## γάρ

γάρ, for, is postpositive: it never stands first in its clause (οὐδὲ γὰρ ἐγένου μαθητής, for you did not even become a disciple).

## Common confusion

In the New Testament εἶδον and some other second aorists often take first-aorist endings (εἶδαν for εἶδον, ἦλθαν); learn the second-aorist forms first and the others are easy to recognize.
