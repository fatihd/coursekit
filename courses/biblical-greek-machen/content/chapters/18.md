# Unit 18 — Present Participles

The present participles, active and middle-passive, and how participles are used.

## Summary

A participle is a verbal adjective: it has tense and voice like a verb and agrees with a noun in gender, number and case like an adjective. The present participle shows action going on at the time of the main verb: λύων ἔρχεται, he comes loosing; λύων ἦλθεν, he came loosing. With the article in the attributive position it is like a relative clause: ὁ ἀπόστολος ὁ λέγων ταῦτα, the apostle who is saying these things. With the article alone it is a noun: ὁ λέγων, he who says. Without the article it usually says when or while: λέγων ταῦτα, while he says these things.

| Acc. pl. | λύοντας | λυούσας | λύοντα |

## λυόμενος, being loosed

λυόμενος, λυομένη, λυόμενον is declined like ἀγαθός. The participle of εἰμί is ὤν, οὖσα, ὄν, being, declined like the endings of λύων.

## Common confusion

λύουσι(ν) is both "they loose" and the dative plural of the participle ("to those who loose"). ὁ λύων is the one who looses; λύων without the article is "while loosing".
