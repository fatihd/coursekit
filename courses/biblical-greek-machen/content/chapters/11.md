# Unit 11 — The Imperfect Active

The imperfect active indicative, the augment, and the imperfect of εἰμί.

## Summary

The imperfect is the past of continuing action: ἔλυον, I was loosing. It is made on the present stem with an augment in front and the secondary endings. Before a consonant the augment is ἐ- (λύω, ἔλυον); a verb beginning with a vowel lengthens it (ἀκούω, ἤκουον; αἴρω, ἦρον; α becomes η). In a compound verb the augment comes after the preposition (ἀποστέλλω, ἀπέστελλον), and a preposition ending in a vowel usually loses it (ἀπο- + ἐ- = ἀπε-). The accent cannot go back past the augment.

## ἔλυον, I was loosing

| Person | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | ἔλυον | ἐλύομεν |
| 2nd | ἔλυες | ἐλύετε |
| 3rd | ἔλυε(ν) | ἔλυον |

The secondary active endings are -ν, -ς, (none), -μεν, -τε, -ν; the first singular and third plural look alike.

## Imperfect of εἰμί

| Person | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | ἤμην | ἦμεν |
| 2nd | ἦς | ἦτε |
| 3rd | ἦν | ἦσαν |

These forms are not enclitic. After οὐκ and some other words the third singular present is ἔστιν (accent on the first syllable): οὐκ ἔστιν, but οὐκ ἐσμέν.

## μέν … δέ

μέν … δέ sets two clauses side by side: τότε μὲν … νῦν δὲ …, then … but now …; μέν is often best left untranslated.
