# Unit 8 — Enclitics, Personal Pronouns, εἰμί

Enclitics and their accents, the personal pronouns, and the present of εἰμί.

## Summary

Some short words lean on the word before them and normally lose their own accent: the unemphatic pronouns μου, μοι, με, σου, σοι, σε and the forms of εἰμί except εἶ. The personal pronouns are ἐγώ (I), σύ (you, sg.) and, for the third person, the oblique cases of αὐτός (him, her, it). δέ, "but" or "and", never stands first in its clause.

## εἰμί, I am

| Person | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | εἰμί | ἐσμέν |
| 2nd | εἶ | ἐστέ |
| 3rd | ἐστί(ν) | εἰσί(ν) |

εἰμί takes a predicate nominative, not an accusative: ὁ ἀπόστολός ἐστιν ἄνθρωπος, the apostle is a man.

## Pronouns

The genitive of a personal pronoun shows possession and stands after the noun: ὁ λόγος μου, my word; ὁ λόγος αὐτοῦ, his word. The emphatic forms ἐμοῦ, ἐμοί, ἐμέ are used after prepositions (δι’ ἐμέ) except πρός με. αὐτός in the nominative means "himself", not "he"; the subject "he" is in the verb.

## Enclitic accents

Before an enclitic a word may gain an extra acute (ὁ ἀπόστολός ἐστιν) and an enclitic itself sometimes keeps its accent (λόγοι εἰσίν). The course accepts either accent of an enclitic and the extra acute or its absence.

## Common confusion

ἐστιν without an accent and ἐστίν with one are the same word; ἔστιν with the accent on the first syllable stands first in a sentence or means "there is".
