# Unit 26 — πᾶς, πολύς, μέγας; Numerals

πᾶς, πολύς and μέγας, the numerals, prepositional phrases and genitives used like adjectives or nouns, and the accusative of extent.

## Summary

πᾶς, πᾶσα, πᾶν, all, every, is declined by the third declension in the masculine and neuter (stem παντ-) and by the first declension in the feminine, nearly like the aorist participle λύσας. Its place decides its meaning. In the predicate position, with a noun that has the article, it means all: πᾶσα ἡ πόλις, all the city. In the attributive position it means whole: ἡ πᾶσα πόλις, the whole city. With a singular noun and no article it means every: πᾶν ὄρος, every mountain. With the article and a participle it makes everyone who …: πᾶς ὁ πιστεύων, everyone who believes; πάντες οἱ πιστεύοντες, all who believe.

πολύς and μέγας are ordinary second- and first-declension adjectives, except for the short forms in the nominative and accusative masculine and neuter singular (πολύς, πολύν, πολύ; μέγας, μέγαν, μέγα).

A prepositional phrase or a genitive placed after the article works like an attributive adjective: οἱ ἐν τῇ πόλει μαθηταί, the disciples who are in the city. Without a noun it works like a noun: οἱ ἐν τῇ πόλει, the people in the city; οἱ τοῦ Ἰησοῦ, those who belong to Jesus. No participle ὄντες is needed.

The accusative answers how far? or how long?: ἔμεινα μίαν ἡμέραν, I remained one day; ἐπορεύθην μετ’ αὐτοῦ στάδιον ἕν, I went with him one furlong.

οὐδείς, οὐδεμία, οὐδέν and μηδείς, μηδεμία, μηδέν, no one, nothing, are declined like εἷς: οὐδείς goes with the indicative, μηδείς with the other moods, the infinitive and the participle. δύο, two, is the same in every case and gender except the dative δυσί(ν). πέντε, ἕξ and the other numbers up to two hundred do not change.

## Common confusion

ἕν (one, neuter) has a rough breathing; ἐν (in) has a smooth one. εἷς (one) and εἰς (into) differ in breathing and accent. οἱ ἐν τῇ πόλει is the people in the city, not in the city alone: the article makes the phrase a noun. πᾶσα ἡ πόλις is all the city, ἡ πᾶσα πόλις the whole city, πᾶσα πόλις every city. ἤ (or) has a smooth breathing; ἡ (the) a rough one.
