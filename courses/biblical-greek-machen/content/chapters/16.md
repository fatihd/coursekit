# Unit 16 — The Aorist and Future Passive

The aorist and future passive indicative, second aorist passives, and deponent verbs with passive forms.

## Summary

The aorist passive and the future passive are both made on the aorist passive stem, the sixth principal part: the verb stem plus θη (λυθη-). The aorist passive has the augment and the active secondary endings (ἐλύθην, I was loosed); the future passive adds σ and the middle primary endings, without augment (λυθήσομαι, I shall be loosed). Before θ a labial becomes φ (ἐπέμφθην), a palatal becomes χ (ἐδιδάχθην, ἤχθην), a dental becomes σ (ἐπείσθην). Some verbs have a second aorist passive without θ: ἐγράφην, I was written.

## ἐλύθην, I was loosed

| Person | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | ἐλύθην | ἐλύθημεν |
| 2nd | ἐλύθης | ἐλύθητε |
| 3rd | ἐλύθη | ἐλύθησαν |

## λυθήσομαι, I shall be loosed

| Person | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | λυθήσομαι | λυθησόμεθα |
| 2nd | λυθήσῃ | λυθήσεσθε |
| 3rd | λυθήσεται | λυθήσονται |

## Deponent verbs

Some deponents have passive, not middle, aorists with active meaning: ἀποκρίνομαι, ἀπεκρίθην, I answered; πορεύομαι, ἐπορεύθην, I went. γίνομαι has both ἐγενόμην and ἐγενήθην, I became, with the same meaning.

## Common confusion

The aorist passive has the active secondary endings (-ν, -ς, none, -μεν, -τε, -σαν), but the future passive has the middle primary endings (λυθήσομαι like λύσομαι).
