# Unit 3 — Present Active Indicative

The present tense of λύω and the verb endings that show the subject.

## Summary

The Greek verb shows its subject by its ending. λύω means I loose; λύομεν, we loose. There is no separate word for "I" or "we" unless it is stressed. The present tense means both "I loose" and "I am loosing".

## Present active indicative of λύω

| Person | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | λύω, I loose | λύομεν, we loose |
| 2nd | λύεις, you (sg.) loose | λύετε, you (pl.) loose |
| 3rd | λύει, he looses | λύουσι(ν), they loose |

Take off the -ω of the first person to find the present stem (λυ-), then add the endings -ω, -εις, -ει, -ομεν, -ετε, -ουσι(ν). Every verb of this lesson goes the same way: βλέπω, βλέπεις, βλέπει …

## Two kinds of "you"

Greek keeps singular and plural "you" apart. The course writes them as you (sg.) and you (pl.); older English used thou and ye, and the typed answers accept those too.

## Verb accent

The accent of a verb goes as far back from the end as the length of the ultima allows: λύομεν, but λύω. This "recessive" accent holds for nearly all verb forms.

## Common confusion

λύουσι and λύουσιν are the same form: the ν (movable ν) is added before a vowel or at the end of a sentence, and the course accepts either.
