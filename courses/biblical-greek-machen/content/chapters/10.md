# Unit 10 — Present Middle and Passive

The present middle and passive of λύω, ὑπό with the genitive of the agent, the dative of means, deponent verbs, compound verbs and the place of οὐ.

## Summary

Greek has three voices. The active and passive are used as in English. The middle shows the subject acting on or for itself; in many verbs its sense cannot be shown in English. In the present the middle and passive have the same forms; in this unit's exercises read them as passive. A deponent verb (ἔρχομαι, γίνομαι, πορεύομαι, ἀποκρίνομαι) has middle or passive forms with an active meaning.

## λύομαι, I am being loosed

| Person | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | λύομαι | λυόμεθα |
| 2nd | λύῃ | λύεσθε |
| 3rd | λύεται | λύονται |

The primary middle endings are -μαι, -σαι, -ται, -μεθα, -σθε, -νται, joined to the stem by the variable vowel ο (before μ and ν) or ε. In the second singular λύεσαι has become λύῃ.

## Agent and means

The personal agent of a passive verb is ὑπό with the genitive: λύεται ὑπὸ τοῦ κυρίου, he is loosed by the Lord. The thing by means of which something is done is the plain dative: ἐγείρονται τῷ λόγῳ τοῦ κυρίου, they are raised by the word of the Lord.

## Compound verbs and οὐ

A preposition prefixed to a verb often adds its own meaning: ἐκ + ἔρχομαι = ἐξέρχομαι, I go out; εἰς + ἔρχομαι = εἰσέρχομαι, I go in. οὐ stands before the word it negates, normally right before the verb; it is οὐκ before a smooth breathing and οὐχ before a rough one.

## Cases with verbs

ἀκούω may take the genitive (ἀκούω τῆς φωνῆς, I hear the voice) or the accusative; ἀποκρίνομαι takes the dative (ἀποκρίνομαι τῷ ἀποστόλῳ, I answer the apostle); ἄρχω, I rule, takes the genitive.
