# Unit 19 — Aorist Participles

The aorist participles, active and middle, and the negatives οὐ and μή.

## Summary

The aorist participle shows action before the time of the main verb: λύσας ἔρχεται, having loosed he comes, after he has loosed he comes; λύσας ἦλθεν, after he had loosed he came. It has no augment. The first aorist active participle is λύσας, λύσασα, λῦσαν; the middle is λυσάμενος. The second aorist participle has the endings of the present participle on the aorist stem, with the accent on the ending: ἰδών, ἰδοῦσα, ἰδόν (having seen), ἐλθών, εἰπών, λαβών; the middle is λιπόμενος.

## οὐ and μή

οὐ negates the indicative; μή negates the other moods, the infinitive and the participle: ὁ μὴ πιστεύων οὐ σῴζεται, he who does not believe is not saved.

## Common confusion

The aorist participle is not "past" in itself; it is before the main verb. With a future main verb it is still before it: ἐλθὼν ὄψομαι, when I have come I shall see.
