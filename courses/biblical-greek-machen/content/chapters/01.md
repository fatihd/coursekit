# Unit 1 — The Alphabet

Read and write the twenty-four letters of the Greek alphabet.

## Summary

Greek has twenty-four letters. Most look like their Latin cousins, but some do not sound the way they look (η is a long e, ρ is r, χ is ch as in Scottish loch). Learn each letter's small form, its capital, its name and its sound, in order, so you can find words in a lexicon.

## Vowels, diphthongs and sigma

The vowels are α, ε, η, ι, ο, υ, ω: ε and ο are always short, η and ω always long, α, ι and υ either. Two vowels pronounced together form a diphthong: αι, ει, οι, υι, αυ, ευ, ου, ηυ. Sigma is written ς at the end of a word and σ everywhere else: σοφός, λόγος. On the course keyboard you always type σ; it becomes ς by itself when the word ends.

## Common confusion

ν (nu) looks like a Latin v, η (eta) like an n, ρ (rho) like a p and χ (chi) like an x. Read them by their Greek names until the shapes stop fooling you. γγ is ng, as in ἄγγελος (angel).
