# Unit 7 — Masculine First Declension; Prepositions

Nouns in -ης like προφήτης, and prepositions with the genitive, dative and accusative.

## Summary

A few first-declension nouns are masculine and end in -ης (προφήτης, μαθητής): genitive singular -ου, like the second declension. Prepositions take fixed cases: the genitive for separation (ἀπό, ἐκ), the dative for rest (ἐν), the accusative for motion toward (εἰς, πρός).

## προφήτης

| Case | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nom. | προφήτης | προφῆται |
| Gen. | προφήτου | προφητῶν |
| Dat. | προφήτῃ | προφήταις |
| Acc. | προφήτην | προφήτας |
| Voc. | προφῆτα | προφῆται |

## Prepositions with more than one case

Learn each preposition together with its case: διά with the genitive is "through", with the accusative "on account of"; μετά with the genitive is "with", with the accusative "after". ἐκ becomes ἐξ before a vowel. A preposition ending in a vowel may drop it before a vowel (δι’ αὐτοῦ, ἀπ’ αὐτοῦ), and before a rough breathing its consonant becomes aspirated (ἀφ’ ὑμῶν, μεθ’ ὑμῶν); the course accepts elided and full forms alike.

## Common confusion

ἐν, εἰς and ἐκ are proclitics without an accent of their own. Do not confuse εἰς (into) with εἶς (one) or ἐν (in) with ἕν (one, neuter).
