# Unit 2 — Gutturals and Consonant Sounds

Several consonants are pronounced in the throat or have special phonological behavior. This unit introduces those groups and the transliteration signs used to keep them distinct.

## Summary

Aleph, he, het, and ayin are the principal gutturals. Resh is not usually called a guttural, but it shares some restrictions with them. Transliteration distinguishes aleph (ʾ) from ayin (ʿ), het (ḥ) from he (h), and emphatic consonants such as tet (ṭ) and tsade (ṣ). Pronunciation varies among reading traditions; consistent transliteration preserves distinctions even when the spoken sounds are reduced.
