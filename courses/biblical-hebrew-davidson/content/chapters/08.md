# Unit 8 — Accents, Pause, and Qere-Kethib

Read the accent marks, pause vowels, and the written-versus-read distinction.

## Summary

The accent signs in the Hebrew Bible do three jobs at once. They mark the stressed (tone) syllable of each word; they act as punctuation, grouping and separating the sense; and they are musical, guiding the chant or cantillation by which the text is read. Alongside the accents stand three reading aids: Metheg (a secondary accent), Maqqeph (a hyphen that binds words), and Pause (the lengthening of vowels at a verse break). Finally, Qere and Kethib record the difference between what is written and what is read.

## The three uses of the accents

The accents first mark the tone-syllable, guiding the pronunciation of each word; the main tone falls usually on the last syllable, though in the segholate nouns (מֶלֶךְ, king) and some verb forms it falls on the next-to-last. Second, they are signs of logical punctuation, like our commas and stops, so they are a running commentary on the sense. Third, they are musical signs, guiding the recitative by which the whole text is read. This last use embraces the other two.

## Metheg

Metheg (מֶתֶג, "bridle") is a small vertical stroke beside a vowel. It marks a secondary accent: the syllable second from the tone, if open, is marked so that its vowel is not hurried over and slurred in the rush toward the tone-syllable. It is rarely used with a vowel in a shut syllable (already safe) or with a shewa (too slight to deserve attention). Metheg can also show that a shewa is vocal, and so distinguish one pronunciation from another.

## Maqqeph

Maqqeph is the hyphen that binds two or more words so they are pronounced, in the rhythmic reading, as one. All the words joined by Maqqeph lose their accent except the last, and in consequence a changeable long vowel may shorten: כֹּל (all) keeps its long o, but כָּל־הָעָם (all the people) shortens it. Maqqeph is used almost invariably with certain short words such as אֵת (object marker) and כֹּל (all).

## Pause

At the middle and end of a Hebrew verse a natural pause occurs, and it changes vowels. A short vowel in the tone-syllable becomes long; occasionally the tone shifts from the last syllable to the next-to-last, which is then lengthened; and a vocal shewa before the tone may rise to a full vowel. The result is that a word may appear in a different vowel-form in pause than in the body of the verse — a change to recognize, not to confuse with a different word.

## Qere and Kethib

Kethib (כְּתִיב, "written") is the consonantal text as it stood, held inviolable. When the punctuators preferred a different reading, they set its consonants in the margin and placed its vowels under the written word. That recommended reading is the Qere (קְרֵי, "read"). A small circle over the word calls attention to the margin. In a few very frequent words — above all the divine name, written with the vowels of אֲדֹנָי (Lord) — the Qere vowels are simply written in the text and no margin is needed.

## Common confusion

Do not confuse Metheg, Maqqeph, and Pause: Metheg guards a vowel, Maqqeph binds words, and Pause lengthens at a verse break. And do not read the written letters as if they were always the spoken word — where a Qere is marked, read the marginal consonants with the vowels written in the text.
