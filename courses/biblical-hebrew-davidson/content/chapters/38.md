# Unit 38 — Lamed-Aleph Verbs

Learn the final-radical aleph and its quiescence.

## Summary

Lamed-Aleph verbs end in aleph. At the end of a syllable the aleph is silent, and the vowel before it is lengthened because the syllable has become effectively open. The class tends to fall together with Lamed-He verbs, and the two are frequently confused in spelling.

## The final aleph

When aleph closes a syllable it is silent after the preceding vowel, which lengthens: מָצָא (he found) from a form in which the aleph is not sounded. In the perfect of active verbs the a is kept throughout; in the perfect of stative verbs, and in the other stems, the vowel is e. All imperfects and imperatives take e before the aleph.

## Confusion with Lamed-He

This class has a strong tendency to adopt the vocalisation, and even the spelling, of Lamed-He verbs — "to heal" may be written with either final letter, and the roots "to call" and "to meet" are sometimes confused. This is a genuine feature of the class, so do not force a rigid distinction in every form.

## Common confusion

Do not read the final aleph as a consonant. It is usually silent and only lengthens the vowel before it. Because of the frequent overlap with Lamed-He, compare the two classes rather than insisting that every form be one or the other.

## Worked example

Take מָצָא ("he found"). The final aleph is silent, and the a before it lengthens because the syllable is effectively open. Read the aleph as lengthening the vowel, not as a consonant, and watch for the overlap with Lamed-He spelling.

## Reading practice

Read and parse. מָצָא הָאִישׁ — "the man found". קָרָא הָעָם — "the people called". The final aleph is silent and lengthens the vowel before it; read it as a vowel-carrier, not a consonant.

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (1914), §38.
