# Unit 38 — Qal Yiqtol and Tenseless Forms

Recognize the Qal prefix conjugation and related forms.

## Summary

The imperfect, called Yiqtol in this course, describes incomplete or future action and is built with prefixes, and usually suffixes as well: יִשְׁמֹר (he will keep), תִּשְׁמֹר (you will keep), אֶשְׁמֹר (I will keep), יִשְׁמְרוּ (they will keep). The prefix marks the person, and the ending marks gender and number. Learning the prefix set is the key to reading this conjugation.

## The prefixes

The third-person masculine singular prefix is י (yiqtol), the third-person feminine and second person use ת (tiqtol), the first person uses א (eqtol), and the plural adds an ending. Because י and ת and א all appear at the start, the first letter of a verb is the first thing to read: it tells you who is acting.

## Tenseless forms

The Yiqtol is not strictly future; it describes action that is not presented as a completed whole — ongoing, repeated, or yet to come. Context decides whether to translate "he keeps", "he will keep", or "he used to keep". The Qatal, by contrast, presents the action as complete.

## Common confusion

Do not translate Yiqtol only as future. Its basic value is incomplete action, so past habitual and general present are also possible. Read the prefix for person, then let the sentence decide the time. Watch that the third-person feminine and the second-person masculine share the same prefix ת and differ only by ending.
