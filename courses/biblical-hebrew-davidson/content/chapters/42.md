# Unit 42 — Jussive and Cohortative

Express wishes for the first, second, and third person.

## Summary

The jussive expresses a wish or mild command for the second or third person, often in a shortened form: יְהִי (let there be). The cohortative adds ־ָה to the first person: אֶשְׁמְרָה (let me keep), נִשְׁמְרָה (let us keep).

## Jussive and cohortative

The jussive expresses a wish about a third or second person, often with a shortened vowel: יְהִי, "let it be". The cohortative adds -āh to the first person to express "let me/let us": אֶשְׁמְרָה.

## Will, prohibition, and emphasis

Davidson describes the jussive and cohortative as ways of marking the speaker's attitude. The jussive is brief and peremptory: it can command, entreat, or dissuade, and it is found in the second and third persons. In a negative prohibition it normally follows אַל; an imperative is not the form used to say “do not.” The cohortative points the speaker's will toward an action—desire, intention, self-encouragement, or, in the first-person plural, exhortation. Its added -āh is a useful clue, but the sense still depends on context.

An imperative can also take -āh for emphasis. Do not automatically call every emphatic imperative a cohortative: the person and function differ. The cohortative is first person; the emphatic imperative still addresses the second person. Davidson notes that the added syllable does not always carry an obvious extra emphasis in translation.

## Common confusion

Keep the jussive apart from the ordinary imperfect: in the strong verb they often look alike, and the context (a wish, a command, אַל before it) decides. Shortened forms like יְהִי are the clearest jussives.
