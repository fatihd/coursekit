# Unit 33 — Conjunctions

Join clauses with or, if, that, so that, and lest.

## Summary

Hebrew joins clauses with a few small words: וְ (and, but), אוֹ (or), אִם (if), כִּי (that, because, when), and for purpose לְמַעַן (so that) and פֶּן (lest). Their sense comes from the construction more than from a fixed gloss.

## Conjunctions

The common conjunctions are אוֹ (or) and אִם (if); "whether … or" is אִם … אוֹ or a doubled form. "But" is very often expressed by simple waw; a stronger "but" is אוּלָם. After a negative, "but" is אִם כִּי. Two more conjunctions introduce purpose and fear, both followed by the imperfect: לְמַעַן "so that" and פֶּן "lest": שְׁמַע לְמַעַן תִּלְמַד (hear, so that you may learn), פֶּן־יִשְׁלַח יָדוֹ (lest he put out his hand). Units 77 and 78 treat conditions and purpose in full.

Conjunctions often work in pairs. Watch for the first member of a construction such as “either … or,” “both … and,” or “neither … nor,” then look for its partner; translating only the first word can leave the relationship unclear. Waw is usually “and,” but Davidson notes that it can carry a contrast equivalent to “but.” The stronger particle אוּלָם makes that contrast explicit. After a negative, אִם כִּי can also introduce “but,” so a particle's function must be read from the construction, not its dictionary gloss alone.

## Common confusion

Do not force one English word onto each conjunction. כִּי can be "that", "because", "when", or "but"; waw can be "and" or "but". Read the clause it opens.

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §49.
