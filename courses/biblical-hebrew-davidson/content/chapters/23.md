# Unit 23 — Suffixes on Prepositions

Read pronoun objects attached to prepositions.

## Summary

The same suffixes on a preposition give its object: לִי (to me), בּוֹ (in him), עִמּוֹ (with him). Some prepositions take the suffixes in the form used with plural nouns: עָלַי (upon me), אֵלָיו (to him), לְפָנֶיךָ (before you).

## Prepositional suffixes

The same suffixes on a preposition give the object of that preposition: לְ becomes לִי (to me), לְךָ (to you), לוֹ (to him); בְּ becomes בִּי (in me), בּוֹ (in it). A few prepositions, like עַל and אֶל, change shape a little before the suffix. Learn the suffixes once and they serve both nouns and prepositions.

## Plural-type suffixes and מִן

עַל, אֶל, עַד, תַּחַת, and לִפְנֵי take the suffixes in the form used with plural nouns, with a -y- before the ending: עָלַי (upon me), עָלָיו (upon him), אֵלֶיךָ (to you), לְפָנַי (before me). The meaning is the same; only the joining vowel differs. מִן doubles itself before a suffix: מִמֶּנִּי (from me), מִמֶּנּוּ (from him, or from us).

## Common confusion

When a suffix is attached to a preposition, it is the object of that preposition, not the subject of a sentence. Thus a form meaning “to him” answers “to whom?”, while a suffix on a noun answers “whose?”. Keep this apart from verbal suffixes, which mark the direct object of an action and are treated later. The suffix itself is a pronoun in each case; its grammatical job comes from the word to which it is attached.

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §19.
