# Unit 77 — Conditional Clauses

Read conditions with אִם, כִּי, לוּ, and לוּלֵא.

## Summary

A condition opens with אִם (if) or כִּי (if, when); the main clause states what follows, often introduced by waw. A condition contrary to fact uses לוּ (if) or, negatively, לוּלֵא (if not).

## Conditions

אִם opens a condition: אִם תִּשְׁמֹר, "if you will keep". The consequence follows in the main clause, often with the conjunction ו or simply by sequence. Read the condition first, then the result, and keep the "if … then …" structure clear even when English would reorder it. לוּ and לוּלֵא state a condition contrary to fact: לוּ חָפֵץ יְהוָה לַהֲמִיתֵנוּ, "if Yahweh had wished to kill us".

## Common confusion

Do not flatten אִם into "if" everywhere: in a question it is "or", and in an oath it is "surely not".

## Source

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §49.
