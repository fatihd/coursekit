# Unit 12 — Qere and Kethib

Tell what is written from what is read.

## Summary

Where the Masoretes preferred a reading different from the written consonants, they left the written text untouched (Kethib, "written") and put the preferred reading (Qere, "read") in the margin, placing its vowels under the written word. A few very common words carry their Qere permanently in the text.

## Qere and Kethib

Kethib (כְּתִיב, "written") is the consonantal text as it stood, held inviolable. When the punctuators preferred a different reading, they set its consonants in the margin and placed its vowels under the written word. That recommended reading is the Qere (קְרֵי, "read"). A small circle over the word calls attention to the margin. In a few very frequent words — above all the divine name, written with the vowels of אֲדֹנָי (Lord) — the Qere vowels are simply written in the text and no margin is needed.

## The divine name

The four letters יהוה are written with vowels borrowed from the word to be read aloud in their place: with those of אֲדֹנָי (Lord) as יְהוָה, or, where אֲדֹנָי already stands before it, with those of אֱלֹהִים (God) as יֱהוִה. The vowels belong to the substitute word, not to the name. Like Davidson, this course writes the name as Yahweh in English.

## Common confusion

Do not pronounce a Kethib with the vowels printed under it. Those vowels belong to the Qere in the margin; written letters and printed vowels together make a form that was never spoken.

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §10.
