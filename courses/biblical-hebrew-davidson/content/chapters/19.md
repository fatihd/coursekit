# Unit 19 — Who, What, and the Relative אֲשֶׁר

Ask who and what, and join a clause to a noun with אֲשֶׁר.

## Summary

מִי (who?) asks about persons and מָה (what?) about things; מָה also exclaims "how …!". The relative אֲשֶׁר (who, which, that) joins a clause to a noun and never changes form; the clause tells you which person or thing is meant.

## Who, what, and which

מִי asks "who?" and מָה asks "what?"; joined to the next word by maqqef, מָה usually becomes מַה־ and doubles the next consonant, and it can also exclaim "how …!": מִי אַתֶּם (who are you?), מַה־טּוֹב הַיּוֹם הַזֶּה (how good this day is!). The relative אֲשֶׁר "who, which, that" opens a clause about a noun and never changes its form: הַיּוֹם אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה אֱלֹהִים (the day which God made). English chooses "who", "whom", "which" or "where" as the clause requires; Hebrew always writes אֲשֶׁר.

## The relative clause

The clause after אֲשֶׁר is complete in itself: הַיֶּלֶד אֲשֶׁר שָׁמַע אֶת־הַקּוֹל (the boy who heard the voice). When the noun plays a part inside that clause, Hebrew often repeats it as a pronoun that English leaves out: הַמָּקוֹם אֲשֶׁר אַתָּה עוֹמֵד עָלָיו, "the place on which you are standing", literally "which you are standing on it".

## Common confusion

Do not look for case in אֲשֶׁר: "who", "whom", "which", and "where" are all אֲשֶׁר, and the words of the clause show the role. And מָה changes its vowel with the next sound (מַה־, מָה, מֶה); all are the same word.
