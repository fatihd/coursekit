# Unit 55 — Pe-Guttural Verbs

Handle a guttural in first position.

## Summary

Pe-Guttural verbs begin with one of the gutturals א, ה, ח, or ע. The guttural affects the vowels and the shewa at the front of the word, because gutturals prefer an a-class vowel and take a composite shewa where a strong verb would take a simple one. The changes are the regular consequences of the guttural rules already learned.

## The first guttural

Where a strong verb would have a simple vocal shewa, a first guttural takes a composite shewa (a ḥateph) — a short vowel written with the shewa. So the second-person plural, the imperative, and the infinitive of a Pe-Guttural verb keep a short vowel beside the guttural instead of a bare shewa.

## Vowel changes

The vowel i before a guttural becomes e, and the short vowel often repeats itself under the guttural as a ḥateph of the same quality. In stative verbs (imperfect in a) the guttural takes e in the imperfect; in active verbs (imperfect in o) it takes a. These are the same guttural rules applied to the front of the word.

## Common confusion

Do not treat Pe-Guttural verbs as a new list of exceptions. They follow the guttural laws — prefer a, take a composite shewa, cannot be doubled — already learned for the writing system. Apply those rules and the forms fall into place without extra memorising.

## Worked example

Take עָמַד ("he stood"). The first guttural ע takes a composite shewa where a strong verb would take a simple one, and the vowel near it opens toward a. Watch for the ḥateph under the first guttural — that is the mark of a Pe-Guttural verb.

## Reading practice

Read and parse. עָמַד הָאִישׁ — "the man stood". עָבַר הָעָם — "the people passed over". Watch for the composite shewa under the first guttural, and the a-vowel beside it, as the marks of a Pe-Guttural verb.
