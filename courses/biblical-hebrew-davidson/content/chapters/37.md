# Unit 37 — Lamed-Guttural Verbs

Handle a guttural in final position.

## Summary

Lamed-Guttural verbs end in a guttural (other than final he and aleph, which are treated separately). A final guttural requires an a sound before it, so the vowel before the last letter opens to patah, and in many forms a helping patah — called furtive — slips in between the vowel and the guttural.

## The final guttural

Before a final guttural, a tone-long e or o is displaced by patah: שָׁלַח (he sent) shows a before the ח where a strong verb would keep o. The guttural itself keeps a silent shewa when it closes the word. This is the first guttural law — every final guttural takes an a before it — applied to the end of the verb.

## The furtive patah

When a long, unchangeable vowel stands before the final guttural, a short a (pathaḥ furtive) is written between them to make the pronunciation possible. Read this furtive patah as a glide into the guttural, not as a full syllable. It appears in forms like the infinitive absolute and the passive participle.

## Common confusion

Do not expect the ordinary vowel pattern before a final guttural. The guttural demands a before it, and the furtive patah is a pronunciation aid, not a grammatical vowel. Read the guttural and the vowel together, and the spelling becomes regular.

## Worked example

Take שָׁלַח ("he sent"). The final ḥet demands an a before it, so the vowel opens to patah where a strong verb would keep o. Every final guttural takes an a before it; the furtive patah is the same rule in a different position.

## Reading practice

Read and parse. שָׁלַח הָאִישׁ — "the man sent". שָׁמַע הָעָם — "the people heard". The a before the final guttural, and the silent shewa on it, are the marks of a Lamed-Guttural verb.
