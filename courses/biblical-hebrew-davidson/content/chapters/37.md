# Unit 37 — Participial Nouns (Third Declension)

Inflect nouns built like the participle, such as כֹּהֵן.

## Summary

A third family of nouns is built like the participle, ending in -ē with an unchangeable vowel before it: כֹּהֵן (priest), אֹיֵב (enemy), שֹׁפֵט (judge). Many nouns with a prefixed מ belong here too: מִשְׁפָּט (judgment), מַלְאָךְ (messenger).

## The participial nouns

A third family ends in -ē with an unchangeable vowel before it: כֹּהֵן (priest), אֹיֵב (enemy), שֹׁפֵט (judge). These follow the verb's law of inflection: with a vocalic suffix the tone-vowel reduces to shewa (כֹּהֲנֵי, priests of), and with a consonantal suffix it shortens. Many words formed with a prefixed m — מִשְׁפָּט (judgment), מַלְאָךְ (messenger) — belong to this family.

## The forms

| Form | כֹּהֵן (priest) | אֹיֵב (enemy) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| plural | כֹּהֲנִים | אֹיְבִים |
| construct plural | כֹּהֲנֵי | אֹיְבֵי |
| with suffix | כֹּהֲנוֹ (his priest) | אֹיְבִי (my enemy), אֹיִבְךָ (your enemy) |

## Common confusion

Do not parse every word of this shape as a verb. A participle can be a verb ("keeping") or a noun ("keeper"); a word like כֹּהֵן is only a noun, but it inflects the same way.

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §30.
