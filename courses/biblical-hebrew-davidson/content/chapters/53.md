# Unit 53 — Agreement and Pronominal Reference

Check subject-predicate and pronominal agreement.

## Summary

A verb or predicate agrees with its subject in person, gender, and number, and a pronoun agrees with its antecedent. When you meet a pronoun or a verb, ask what it refers to and confirm the agreement; a mismatch usually means the referent has been misread. Agreement is a built-in cross-check that keeps reading accurate.

## Agreement


| Subject | Verb |
| --- | --- |
| הָאִישׁ (the man) | שָׁמַר (he kept) |
| הָאִשָּׁה (the woman) | שָׁמְרָה (she kept) |
| הָאֲנָשִׁים (the men) | שָׁמְרוּ (they kept) |


## Subject agreement

הָאִישׁ שָׁמַר is "the man kept" (masculine singular subject, masculine singular verb); הָאִשָּׁה שָׁמְרָה is "the woman kept"; הָאֲנָשִׁים שָׁמְרוּ is "the men kept". The verb's ending points back to its subject, so a verb can confirm who the subject is even across intervening words.

## Pronominal reference

A pronoun stands for a noun already mentioned: הָאִישׁ בָּא וְהוּא עָמַד, "the man came and he stood". Check the pronoun's gender and number against its antecedent; if they do not match, the pronoun refers to someone else or you have misread the antecedent.

## Common confusion

Do not ignore agreement as a detail. When a verb ending does not fit the noun you assumed, reconsider the subject. Collective nouns (like עַם, "people") sometimes take a plural verb by sense, and a few nouns have unexpected gender — learn those as you meet them rather than forcing the agreement.

## Source

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (1914), §12–13, §16.
