# Unit 62 — Nouns from Hollow Roots

Read nouns built on Ayin-Waw and Ayin-Yod roots.

## Summary

Nouns formed from roots whose middle letter is waw or yod show that weak radical through their vowel patterns: אוֹר (light), מָוֶת (death), קוֹל (voice), and מָקוֹם (place). Many have stable long vowels, but not every form is identical: מָוֶת has the shorter construct מוֹת (death of). Recognize the pattern family, then learn the attested construct, plural, and suffix forms rather than assuming one vowel shape never changes.

## Hollow nouns

Words derived from roots with a middle waw or yod — such as מָוֶת (death, from מות), אוֹר (light, from אור), קוֹל (voice, from קול), מָקוֹם (place, from קום), and עַיִן (eye, from עין) — preserve a trace of the weak radical in their forms. Davidson distinguishes several patterns: some nouns keep an unchangeable vowel, while others prefer a shorter form in construct or with suffixes. Ayin-Waw and Ayin-Yod nouns also differ in some vowel developments, so compare the attested forms instead of treating all hollow nouns as one paradigm.

## Their inflection

The weak radical often makes these nouns shorter than their verb citation form, but each noun's forms must be learned as a set. מָוֶת becomes מוֹת in the construct; קוֹל remains קוֹל in the construct and forms קוֹלוֹ (his voice), while עַיִן has the dual עֵינַיִם (eyes). In plural and suffix forms, a shortened or contracted stem may appear. Use the listed forms and context to identify the pattern.

## Common confusion

Do not force every hollow noun into an invariant-vowel rule. The family is defined by the weak middle radical and includes more than one pattern. A segholate has a helping vowel that can disappear before an ending; hollow nouns instead reflect a lost or contracted middle waw/yod. Compare absolute, construct, and suffixed forms to tell the patterns apart.

## Source

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §41.
