# Unit 6 — Vowel Letters and Vowel Points Together

Read words whose vowel is written both by a letter and by a point.

## Summary

The vowel points were added to a consonantal text that already used ו, י, and final ה for many long vowels. Out of reverence the Masoretes left those letters in place, so a word like קוֹל (voice) or שִׁירָה (song) shows its vowel twice: once by the letter and once by the point. A syllable written with its vowel letter is called full (plene); written without it, defective.

## Two layers of vowels

Before the points existed, קול was read qôl and שירה šîrāh: the ו and the י were the only vowel signs. The points alone would have written these words קֹל and שִׁרָה. Because the consonants were not altered, the points were added to them as they stood, and the vowel is now marked twice: in קוֹל the holem and the ו both say ô; in שִׁירָה the hiriq and the י both say î. Read the pair as one vowel.

## Full and defective spelling

The same word may be written with or without its vowel letter: חֵיק and חֵק (bosom), קוֹל and קֹל (voice). With the letter the spelling is full (scriptio plena); without it, defective (scriptio defectiva). The pronunciation is the same. The Hebrew Bible is not consistent, and one word can appear both ways, so look up a defective form as if the vowel letter were there.

## Where the point goes

In a full spelling the vowel point stays under the consonant it follows; only holem sits on its ו: חִיל (to writhe), חֵיל (rampart), but חוֹל (sand). Where ו is a consonant, a second ו as vowel letter is usually left out: מִצְוֹת (commandments), not מִצְווֹת. And one word seldom uses the same vowel letter in two syllables running: שִׁרִים for שִׁירִים (songs).

## Common confusion

Do not read a vowel letter after its point as an extra syllable. In קוֹל there is one vowel, ô, written twice; the word is one syllable, qôl.
