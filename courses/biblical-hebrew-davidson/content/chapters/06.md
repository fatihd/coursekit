# Unit 6 — Syllables and Shewa

Divide words into syllables and distinguish vocal from silent shewa.

## Summary

A Hebrew syllable normally opens with a consonant and may be open (ending in a vowel) or closed (ending in a consonant). The shewa (two vertical dots) is vocal when it carries a very short vowel at the start of a syllable, and silent when it simply closes a syllable and has no sound. Dividing a word correctly into syllables tells you which shewas to pronounce and how long each vowel is — the foundation for accurate reading.

## Shewa


| Kind | Where | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vocal | under a consonant at the start of a syllable | דְּבָרִים (dəḇārîm) |
| Silent | closing a syllable | מִשְׁפָּט (mišpāṭ) |


## Syllable structure

One consonant usually begins each syllable. A syllable with a consonant followed by a vowel and nothing more is open (like the first syllable of דָּבָר, dā); a syllable that ends in a consonant is closed (like the second, bār). Hebrew tends to prefer open syllables, which is why a silent shewa appears where a syllable closes.

## Vocal and silent shewa

A shewa at the start of a word, or under a letter after a long vowel, is usually vocal and is pronounced as a very short e. A shewa that closes a syllable, especially after a short vowel, is silent. Under a guttural, a composite shewa carries an audible short vowel. Reading the two correctly keeps syllables distinct.

## Common confusion

The same two dots can be silent or sounded, so never guess from the mark alone; decide from the syllable. If you can divide the word so the shewa closes a syllable, it is silent; if it must open a syllable, it is vocal. Practise dividing before pronouncing.

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §5–6.
