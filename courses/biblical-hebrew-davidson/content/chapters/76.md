# Unit 76 — Relative Clauses

Read clauses introduced by אֲשֶׁר and כִּי.

## Summary

A relative clause describes a noun and is introduced by אֲשֶׁר; כִּי introduces a clause that completes a verb of saying, seeing, or knowing ("that"). Read each such clause as one unit with the word it belongs to.

## Relative clauses

אֲשֶׁר introduces a clause that describes a noun: הָאִישׁ אֲשֶׁר בָּא, "the man who came". כִּי can mean "that" after verbs of saying and knowing: אָמַר כִּי בָּא, "he said that he came". Read the relative clause as a unit attached to its noun or verb.

## Common confusion

Do not translate כִּי with only one English word. It means "that", "for", "because", or "when" depending on context, so let the sentence decide. And do not break a relative clause at the wrong place: אֲשֶׁר reaches forward to its verb, so read to the end of the clause before translating.

## Source

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §13.
