# Unit 10 — Gender and Number

Recognize noun and adjective gender, number, and agreement.

## Summary

Every Hebrew noun is masculine or feminine, and singular, plural, or (rarely) dual. Gender is grammatical, not natural: it is a property of the word, so the word for "book" is masculine and the word for "land" is feminine regardless of what they name. There is no neuter. The most reliable way to know a noun's gender is to learn it with the word, because the endings only hint at it.

Feminine nouns very often end in -āh (מַלְכָּה, "queen") or in -t, but many feminine nouns have neither ending (אֶרֶץ, "land"). Masculine nouns usually have no special ending in the singular. The regular masculine plural is -îm (מְלָכִים, "kings"), and the regular feminine plural is -ôt (מְלָכוֹת, "queens"), but many common words are irregular, so the plural must be learned with each noun.

Adjectives agree with the noun they describe in gender and number: a masculine singular noun takes a masculine singular adjective, and so on. When you meet a noun and adjective together, compare their forms before you translate the phrase.

## Gender and number endings

| Form | Typical ending | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| masculine singular | — | מֶלֶךְ (king) |
| feminine singular | -āh / -t | מַלְכָּה (queen) |
| masculine plural | -îm | מְלָכִים (kings) |
| feminine plural | -ôt | מְלָכוֹת (queens) |

## The dual

A small group of nouns uses a special dual ending, -ayim, for things that naturally come in pairs. The hand is יָד (singular) but יָדַיִם (two hands); the eye is עַיִן but עֵינַיִם (two eyes). The dual is limited and mostly fixed to body parts and a few measures, so you need not produce it freely — only recognise it.

## Agreement

A Hebrew adjective normally follows its noun and copies its gender and number: מֶלֶךְ טוֹב, "a good king", but מַלְכָּה טוֹבָה, "a good queen". This agreement is the main clue that a word is an adjective rather than another noun. In the next units you will add the article, which adds a third point of agreement, definiteness.

## Common confusion

Do not guess gender from the English meaning, and do not guess the plural from the ending alone. A word ending in -im is usually masculine plural, but a few are feminine; a word ending in -ôt is usually feminine plural. Learn each noun as a set — singular, gender, and plural together — and treat the endings as hints, not rules.

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (1914), §16.
