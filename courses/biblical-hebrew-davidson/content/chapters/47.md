# Unit 47 — Verb Suffixes

Recognize object and pronominal suffixes on verbs.

## Summary

A verb may take a pronominal suffix that marks the direct object of the action, not the subject. שְׁמָרוֹ means "he kept him", and שְׁמָרַנִי "he kept me". The suffix's person, gender, and number identify the one acted upon, while the verb's own ending still marks the subject. These suffixes are the same in form as the possessive suffixes on nouns.

## Object suffixes

The pronominal suffixes attached to a verb express the direct object, and they are the same suffixes that nouns take for possession. So שְׁמָרוֹ ("he kept him") and בֵּיתוֹ ("his house") share the suffix -ô. Learn the suffix set once and it serves nouns, prepositions, and verbs alike.

## Reading the suffix

To parse a suffixed verb, first name the verb's own stem, conjugation, and subject from its prefix and ending, then name the object from the suffix. The suffix does not change the subject; it adds the one acted upon. Distinguish a subject ending from an object suffix by asking whether the verb already has its subject marked.

## Common confusion

Do not confuse a subject ending with an object suffix. The verb's person ending marks the doer; a following suffix marks the one done to. When a suffix appears on a form that already has its subject, the suffix is the object. Read both, in order, before translating.

## Worked example

Take שְׁמָרוֹ ("he kept him"). The verb's own ending marks the subject "he", and the suffix -ô marks the object "him". Read the subject from the verb and the object from the suffix, in that order, before translating.

## Reading practice

Read and parse. שְׁמָרוֹ — "he kept him" (subject "he", object "him"). שְׁמָרַנִי — "he kept me". Read the subject from the verb's ending and the object from the suffix, in that order.

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (1914), §31.
