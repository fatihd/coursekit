# Unit 2 — Gutturals and Consonant Sounds

Recognize the guttural letters and use a consistent transliteration.

## Summary

Four letters are called gutturals: א (aleph), ה (he), ח (het), and ע (ayin); the letter ר (resh) shares some of their behaviour even though it is not classed with them. Gutturals have two practical effects. First, they dislike a simple vocal shewa and usually take a composite shewa, one that carries a short vowel sound, so the mouth has a vowel to move through. Second, they prefer an a-class vowel next to them, so vowels often shift toward a or the short a-like sounds when a guttural is near.

## Gutturals


| Letter | Name | Quality |
| --- | --- | --- |
| א | Aleph | glottal stop (often silent) |
| ה | He | h |
| ח | Het | strong h (ḥ) |
| ע | Ayin | deep guttural (ʼ) |
| ר | Resh | treated like a guttural for shewa |


## The guttural letters

Learn the gutturals as a group and treat them as a unit when you read. א is a glottal stop that is usually silent in modern pronunciation; ה is a plain h; ח is a strong h from the back of the throat; ע is a deep guttural made deep in the throat. Because these four letters sit at the back of the mouth, the vowel beside them naturally opens into an a sound, and a shewa under them becomes a short vowel shewa rather than a silent stroke.

## Transliteration

Use one transliteration system and keep it steady. This course writes ʼ for both א and ע, ḥ for ח, š for שׁ, ś for שׂ, ṣ for צ, and ṭ for ט. The mark ʼ does not distinguish aleph from ayin; if you need that distinction later, write ʿ for ע and ʾ for א. The point of transliteration is only to let you record and check sounds; the goal is to read the Hebrew signs directly.

## Common confusion

Do not treat gutturals as optional or silent letters to ignore. Even when א is not pronounced, it still occupies a place in the word and affects the vowels around it. When you meet a composite shewa (a shewa joined to a short vowel) under a letter, that is usually a sign that the letter is a guttural; expect the following vowel to be a-class.
