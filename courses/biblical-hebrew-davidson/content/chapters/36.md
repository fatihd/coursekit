# Unit 36 — Ayin-Guttural Verbs

Handle a guttural in middle position.

## Summary

Ayin-Guttural verbs have a guttural as the middle radical. The middle guttural cannot be doubled, and it prefers an a-class vowel. This shows most clearly in the intensive stems — Piel, Pual, and Hitpael — where the expected doubling of the middle radical is replaced by a lengthened preceding vowel.

## The middle guttural

In the Qal imperfect and imperative the middle guttural prefers a, so the vowel before it opens to a. In the perfect of Piel the vowel is often a as well. These are the guttural's preference for the a sound working in the middle of the word.

## The intensive forms

Because a guttural cannot be doubled, Piel, Pual, and Hitpael omit the dagesh from the middle radical, and the preceding short vowel is lengthened to compensate. So "he blessed" (Piel of ברך) is בֵּרֵךְ, with a long e before the guttural instead of a doubled resh. The stem is still recognisable by its prefix and vowel pattern.

## Common confusion

Do not mistake an Ayin-Guttural Piel for another stem just because the doubling is missing. The guttural rule explains it: where Piel would double, the guttural forces a long vowel instead. Name the stem first, then apply the guttural law.

## Worked example

Take בֵּרֵךְ ("he blessed", Piel of ברך). The middle radical resh is a guttural and cannot be doubled, so the vowel before it lengthens to ē instead. The stem is still Piel — the i and the long ē — but the doubling is replaced by the lengthened vowel.

## Reading practice

Read and parse. שָׁאַל הָאִישׁ — "the man asked". בֵּרֵךְ אֶת־הָעָם — "he blessed the people" (Piel of a guttural root). Where Piel would double, the guttural forces a lengthened vowel instead.
