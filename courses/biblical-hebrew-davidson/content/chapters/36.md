# Unit 36 — The Qal Participle

Read the active participle as verb, adjective, and noun.

## Summary

The participle is a different kind of form. It is built on a vowel pattern (the active participle looks like שֹׁמֵר, "keeping" or "one who keeps") and behaves partly like an adjective and partly like a noun. Where the Qatal reports a finished action, the participle describes an ongoing action or a role. Learn the Qatal person suffixes and the participle's four gender/number forms, because most reading depends on telling these two apart.

## Qal participles

| Gender | Active |
| --- | --- |
| masculine | שֹׁמֵר (keeping) |
| feminine | שֹׁמֶרֶת (keeping) |
| masculine plural | שֹׁמְרִים (keeping) |
| feminine plural | שֹׁמְרוֹת (keeping) |

## Participle as agent and adjective

The active participle names the one who performs the action, so שֹׁמֵר is "keeping", "a keeper", or "a guard" depending on context. Like an adjective, it changes for gender and number to agree with the person or thing it describes. It also takes the definite article and pronominal suffixes the way a noun does — behaviour you will see again when you study the noun phrase and the verb stems.

## Common confusion

Do not confuse the third-person feminine Qatal, קָטְלָה ("she killed"), with a feminine participle such as קֹטֶלֶת ("killing"). The Qatal marks a completed action with a suffix; the participle marks an ongoing action with a vowel pattern. Check the vowel pattern and the ending, then decide whether the form reports an event or describes an agent.

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §20.
