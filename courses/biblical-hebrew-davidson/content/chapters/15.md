# Unit 15 — Independent Pronouns

Read the personal pronouns by person, gender, and number.

## Summary

The independent personal pronoun stands alone as the subject of a sentence: אֲנִי (I), אַתָּה (you), הוּא (he). It marks person (first, second, third), gender (masculine or feminine), and number (singular or plural). Hebrew uses the independent pronoun for emphasis, for contrast, and as the subject of a verbless sentence, but because the verb already carries its own person ending, the pronoun is not needed to make a verb say who is acting.

## Independent pronouns


| Person | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | אֲנִי (I) | אֲנַחְנוּ (we) |
| 2 m | אַתָּה (you) | אַתֶּם (you) |
| 2 f | אַתְּ (you) | אַתֶּן (you) |
| 3 m | הוּא (he) | הֵם (they) |
| 3 f | הִיא (she) | הֵנָּה (they) |


## Person, gender, and number

The first person is the speaker (אֲנִי, אֲנַחְנוּ); the second is the one spoken to (אַתָּה, אַתְּ, אַתֶּם); the third is the one spoken about (הוּא, הִיא, הֵם, הֵנָּה). The second and third persons distinguish masculine from feminine in both singular and plural; the first person does not. Learn the whole table so the forms are automatic.

## Using the pronoun

Because a verb carries its person in its ending, the separate pronoun usually adds emphasis: אֲנִי שָׁמַרְתִּי stresses "I myself kept". In a verbless sentence the pronoun supplies the missing verb: הוּא מֶלֶךְ is "he is a king". Notice the pronoun agrees with the noun or verb it stands for.

## Common confusion

Do not add a separate pronoun to every verb; that over-translates. Reserve the independent pronoun for emphasis or for sentences with no verb. Also do not confuse הוּא (he) with הִיא (she): the difference is the vowel and the final letter, so read carefully.

## Source

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §12.
