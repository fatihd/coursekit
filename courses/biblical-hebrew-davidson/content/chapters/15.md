# Unit 15 — Demonstratives and Waw

Use demonstratives and the conjunction waw in simple clauses.

## Summary

The demonstratives זֶה (this, masculine) and זֹאת (this, feminine), and אֵלֶּה (these) point to a nearby person or thing. Like adjectives they usually follow the noun and, when the noun is definite, they are made definite as well. The conjunction ו (waw) means "and"; it prefixes the next word and changes its short vowel depending on what follows. Both are workhorses of simple clauses.

## Demonstratives


| Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| זֶה | this (masculine) |
| זֹאת | this (feminine) |
| אֵלֶּה | these |


## Demonstratives

זֶה and זֹאת agree with their noun in gender, אֵלֶּה in number. Because the demonstrative is definite by nature, a definite noun takes a definite demonstrative: הָאִישׁ הַזֶּה (this man), הָאִשָּׁה הַזֹּאת (this woman). They can also stand alone: זֶה טוֹב, "this is good".

## Waw

The conjunction ו attaches to the front of a word: דָּבָר (word) becomes וּדְבַר (and a word). Before a consonant its vowel is usually a vocal shewa (וְ), but before a shewa, a guttural, or a labial consonant it becomes û or takes another form. These small changes are pronunciation only; the meaning stays "and".

## Common confusion

Do not read ו as a full word with a fixed vowel. Its vowel adapts to the next sound, so וּ, וַ, וֶ, וֹ all occur as the same conjunction in different positions. Recognise the prefixed ו as "and" and let the following word determine how it is read.

## Source

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §13, §15.
