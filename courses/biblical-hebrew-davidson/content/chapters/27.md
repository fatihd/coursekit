# Unit 27 — Niphal

Learn the Niphal stem, the simple reflexive and passive.

## Summary

Niphal is the simple reflexive and passive. Its characteristic letter is nun: in the perfect the prefix ni- is added to the stem, and in the imperfect the prefix is yin-, whose nun then assimilates to the next consonant and doubles it. So קָטַל (he killed) becomes נִקְטַל (he was killed), and the imperfect יִקָּטֵל shows the doubled middle letter. Niphal is inflected exactly like Qal, so once you know the Qal you can build it freely.

## The form

The perfect prefixes ni-, closing the first syllable; the imperfect prefixes yin-, and the nun assimilates so that the first root consonant is doubled. The imperative and infinitive construct add a secondary he on the front (as if by analogy with the Hiphil), giving the form הִקָּטֵל. The participle is like the perfect but with a long first vowel.

## The meanings

Niphal is properly the reflexive of Qal — נִשְׁמַר, "he kept himself, he was on guard" — and it also expresses reciprocal action (two parties acting on one another) and, most commonly, the passive of Qal, as "he was broken", "he was buried". The participle sometimes has a gerundive force, meaning "to be desired, desirable".

## The agent

When a Niphal is passive, the agent — the one by whom the action is done — is usually expressed by the preposition לְ. Read it as "by": נִבְחַר לוֹ, "chosen by him". This small idiom recurs, so recognise it as you read.

## Common confusion

Do not lump Niphal together with Hitpael. They are different forms with different histories: Niphal is the simple reflexive/passive built on the Qal, while Hitpael is the reflexive of the intensive. Their prefixes and vowels differ, and their meanings should be translated from context, not from a single label.

## Worked example

Take נִכְתַּב ("it was written"). The nun prefix marks Niphal, and the imperfect would be יִכָּתֵב, with the doubled kaf from the assimilated nun. Translate it as the passive of the simple stem: "it was written", not "it wrote itself". The clue to Niphal is always the nun prefix in the perfect and the doubled first radical in the imperfect.

## Reading practice

Read these aloud and parse each form. נִכְתַּב הַסֵּפֶר — "the book was written" (Niphal perfect). נִשְׁמַר הָאִישׁ — "the man kept himself, was on guard" (reflexive). In each, the nun prefix and the doubled radical in the imperfect are the clues to Niphal.
