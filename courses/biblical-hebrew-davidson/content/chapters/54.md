# Unit 54 — Waw, Relative, and Circumstantial Clauses

Use waw and relative clauses to connect sentences.

## Summary

Waw connects words and clauses with "and", and a relative clause introduced by אֲשֶׁר (who, which, that) or כִּי (that) modifies a noun or completes a verb. Recognise where a relative clause begins and ends, and translate the relative particle appropriately. These connectors are what turn lists of words into continuous narrative.

## Relative and waw


| Clause | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| הָאִישׁ אֲשֶׁר בָּא | the man who came |
| וַיֹּאמֶר | and he said |


## Waw

The conjunction ו links nouns, phrases, and whole clauses. At the start of a clause it often carries the narrative along: וַיֹּאמֶר, "and he said". The vowel of ו adjusts to the next word, but its joining force is constant. Distinguish simple "and" from the narrative "and then" by context.

## Relative clauses

אֲשֶׁר introduces a clause that describes a noun: הָאִישׁ אֲשֶׁר בָּא, "the man who came". כִּי can mean "that" after verbs of saying and knowing: אָמַר כִּי בָּא, "he said that he came". Read the relative clause as a unit attached to its noun or verb.

## Common confusion

Do not translate כִּי with only one English word. It means "that", "for", "because", or "when" depending on context, so let the sentence decide. And do not break a relative clause at the wrong place: אֲשֶׁר reaches forward to its verb, so read to the end of the clause before translating.

## Source

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §13, §15.
