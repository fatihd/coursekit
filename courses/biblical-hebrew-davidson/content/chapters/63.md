# Unit 63 — Double-Ayin (Geminate) Verbs

Recognize doubled (geminate) verbs.

## Summary

Double-Ayin (geminate) verbs have the same letter as their second and third radicals, so the root collapses into a doubled consonant that behaves as a single strengthened letter. סָבַב (he surrounded, from סבב) becomes יָסֹב (he will surround), and מָדַד (he measured) becomes יָמֹד. The two identical letters often merge into one.

## The doubled radical

A geminate verb writes its second and third radicals as one doubled letter. In the perfect the doubling shows — סָבַב — but in the imperfect the two letters collapse, and the form shortens to יָסֹב. The root is recovered from the doubled consonant in the perfect.

## The forms

The imperfect of the geminate is built on a shortened stem, and the other parts follow it. Because the middle and final radicals are identical, the verb looks like a hollow verb at a glance, but the perfect preserves the doubled consonant, which identifies the class.

## Common confusion

Do not confuse a geminate verb with a Pe-Nun verb: both can show doubling, but in a geminate the middle and final radicals are genuinely identical, while in Pe-Nun the doubling comes from a dropped nun. Compare the perfect to see which is which.

## Worked example

Take סָבַב ("he surrounded", from סבב) and יָסֹב ("he will surround"). The two identical letters collapse into a single doubled consonant in the perfect and a short form in the imperfect. The doubled consonant in the perfect is the identifying mark.

## Reading practice

Read and parse. סָבַב הָעָם — "the people surrounded". יָסֹב — "he will surround". The doubled consonant in the perfect collapses in the imperfect; the doubled letter identifies the geminate root.

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §42.
