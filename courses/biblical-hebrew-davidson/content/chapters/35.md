# Unit 35 — Qal Qatal

Learn the Qal perfect and its person endings.

## Summary

The perfect, called Qatal in this course, is the basic suffix conjugation of the Hebrew verb. It states an action as a completed whole — "he killed", "she kept", "they wrote" — and is the form a dictionary normally lists. The Qatal is built by attaching person endings to the root: the simplest form, קָטַל ("he killed"), is the third-person masculine singular, and every other person adds a suffix to the base.

## Qal Qatal of קָטַל

| Person | Form |
| --- | --- |
| 3ms | קָטַל |
| 3fs | קָטְלָה |
| 2ms | קָטַלְתָּ |
| 2fs | קָטַלְתְּ |
| 1cs | קָטַלְתִּי |
| 3cp | קָטְלוּ |
| 2mp | קְטַלְתֶּם |
| 2fp | קְטַלְתֶּן |
| 1cp | קָטַלְנוּ |

## The Qatal suffixes

The third-person masculine singular carries no suffix; it is the bare root in its Qatal vowel pattern. The feminine singular adds -āh (קָטְלָה), the second person adds -tā / -t / -tem, the first person adds -tî, and the plural adds -û or -nû. Reading the suffix tells you who did the action, which is why Hebrew can omit the separate pronoun: קָטַל already means "he killed".

## Common confusion

Do not add a pronoun the ending already gives: שָׁמַרְתִּי is "I kept". The bare form קָטַל is a full sentence, "he killed", as well as the dictionary form of the verb.

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §20.
