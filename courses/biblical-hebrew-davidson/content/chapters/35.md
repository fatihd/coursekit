# Unit 35 — Pe-Aleph Verbs

Learn the first-radical aleph that quiesces in the imperfect.

## Summary

Pe-Aleph is a small sub-class of Pe-Guttural. Its single special feature is that in the Qal imperfect the first-radical aleph quiesces (falls silent) into the vowel o: אָכַל becomes יֹאכַל, and אָמַר becomes יֹאמַר. In every other respect these verbs behave like first-guttural verbs.

## The quiescent aleph

In the imperfect Qal the aleph is absorbed into an o, so the prefix and the root's first letter merge into a single o: יֹאמַר (he will say) instead of a hypothetical yiʾmar. This is a small, closed class, and only a handful of very common verbs belong to it.

## The five verbs

The verbs of this class are few and frequent: אָבַד (to perish), אָכַל (to eat), אָמַר (to say), אָבָה (to be willing), and אָפָה (to bake). Learn them with their imperfects as a fixed list, and the rest of the class is covered.

## Common confusion

Do not look for a separate aleph in the imperfect of these verbs. The o of יֹאמַר is the trace of the aleph. A few verbs also have a regular Pe-Guttural imperfect beside the quiescent one, so when in doubt compare the forms and the context.

## Worked example

Take אָכַל ("he ate") and its imperfect יֹאכַל ("he will eat"). The aleph quiesces into o, so the prefix and the root's first letter merge. The o in the imperfect — not a separate aleph — is the mark of this small class.

## Reading practice

Read and parse. אָכַל אֶת־הַלֶּחֶם — "he ate the bread". יֹאמַר הָאִישׁ — "the man will say". The imperfect's o (יֹאכַל, יֹאמַר) is the quiescent aleph; there is no separate aleph to read.
