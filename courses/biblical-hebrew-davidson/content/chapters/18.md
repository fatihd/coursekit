# Unit 18 — The Construct State and the Directional Ending

Join nouns as "X of Y", and mark motion toward a place.

## Summary

Two nouns joined as "X of Y" form a construct chain: the first noun is in the construct state, shortened and unstressed, and never takes the article; the chain is definite if its last noun is. A separate ending, unstressed ־ָה, marks motion toward a place. Davidson treats both under the cases of the noun.

## The construct state

Hebrew says "the word of the king" by joining two nouns: דְּבַר הַמֶּלֶךְ. The first noun is in the construct state: it loses its stress, its vowels often shorten (דָּבָר → דְּבַר, plural דְּבָרִים → דִּבְרֵי), and it never takes the article. The whole chain is definite when its last noun is definite: דְּבַר הַמֶּלֶךְ "the word of the king", דְּבַר מֶלֶךְ "a word of a king". An adjective stands after the whole chain: יוֹם יְהוָה הַגָּדוֹל (the great day of Yahweh). The endings change: feminine ־ָה becomes ־ַת (תּוֹרָה → תּוֹרַת), masculine plural ־ִים and dual ־ַיִם become ־ֵי (סוּסִים → סוּסֵי, יָדַיִם → יְדֵי). כֹּל "all" is usually construct כָּל־: כָּל־הָאָרֶץ (all the earth). Unit 73 takes longer chains further.

## The directional ending

An unstressed ־ָה added to a noun marks motion toward it: אֶרֶץ → אַרְצָה (to the ground), מִצְרַיִם → מִצְרַיְמָה (to Egypt), צָפוֹן → צָפוֹנָה (northward), הָהָר → הָהָרָה (toward the mountain). Do not confuse it with the feminine ending ־ָה, which is stressed.

## Common confusion

Do not give the first noun "the" because the chain is definite: in דְּבַר הַמֶּלֶךְ (the word of the king) only the last noun has the article. And do not mistake the directional ־ָה for a feminine ending: אַרְצָה (to the ground) is stressed on its first syllable.
