# Unit 18 — Demonstratives

Point to things near and far with this, that, these, and those.

## Summary

זֶה (this, masculine), זֹאת (this, feminine), and אֵלֶּה (these) point to something near; הוּא, הִיא (that) and הֵם, הֵן (those) point to something farther away. After a noun they behave like adjectives and take the article when the noun has it: הָאִישׁ הַזֶּה (this man), הַיּוֹם הַהוּא (that day). Before a noun, without the article, they make a sentence: זֶה הָאִישׁ (this is the man).

## Demonstratives

| Form | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| זֶה | this (masculine) |
| זֹאת | this (feminine) |
| אֵלֶּה | these |
| הוּא / הִיא | that (masculine / feminine) |
| הֵם / הֵן | those (masculine / feminine) |

## Demonstratives

זֶה and זֹאת agree with their noun in gender, אֵלֶּה in number. Because the demonstrative is definite by nature, a definite noun takes a definite demonstrative: הָאִישׁ הַזֶּה (this man), הָאִשָּׁה הַזֹּאת (this woman). They can also stand alone: זֶה טוֹב, "this is good".

## Common confusion

Watch the article. הָאִישׁ הַזֶּה is "this man", a phrase; זֶה הָאִישׁ is "this is the man", a sentence. And הוּא can be "he" or "that": with the article after a definite noun, as in הַיּוֹם הַהוּא, it is "that".

## Source

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §13.
