# Unit 29 — Pual

Learn the Pual stem, the passive of Piel, and contrast it with the Qal passive.

## Summary

Pual is the passive of Piel in its various senses. Where Piel has the i–e vowels (קִטֵּל), Pual has u–a (קֻטַּל, "he was killed utterly"), with the same doubled middle radical. It inflects like Qal and is much rarer than Piel, so recognition is the main task.

## The form

Pual keeps the doubled middle radical of Piel but changes the vowels to u–a: קֻטַּל (perfect) and יְקֻטַּל (imperfect). Its participles follow the imperfect and begin with m. Once you know Piel, Pual is a small step.

## Qal passive and Pual

Hebrew once had a passive Qal, whose only surviving traces are the passive participle and a few forms. Pual is a different thing: the passive of the intensive Piel. This matters because the two are easy to confuse — a passive-looking form might be a Qal passive rather than a Pual. Check the stem: if the active would be Piel (with its doubled middle letter and i–e vowels), the passive is Pual; if the active would be Qal, the passive is the old Qal passive, not Pual.

## Common confusion

The distinction between the Qal passive and Pual is often lost in a quick glance, because both look passive. The test is the stem of the active verb. When the meaning is simple and passive, it is the Qal passive; when it is the passive of an intensive or factitive idea, it is Pual. Davidson notes that a few forms are best taken as old Qal passives rather than Pual or Hophal.

## Worked example

Take קֻטַּל ("he was killed utterly"). The u–a vowels and the doubled middle letter mark Pual, the passive of Piel קִטֵּל. Ask which active it is the passive of: if the active would be an intensive Piel, the passive is Pual. This is how you tell it from the old Qal passive.

## Reading practice

Read and parse. שֻׁלַּח הָעֶבֶד — "the servant was sent" (Pual). The u–a vowels with the doubled letter mark Pual, the passive of Piel. Ask which intensive active it is the passive of before translating.
