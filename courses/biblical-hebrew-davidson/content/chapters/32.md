# Unit 32 — Hitpael

Learn the Hitpael stem and its reflexive meanings.

## Summary

Hitpael is the reflexive stem, formed by prefixing hith- to the Piel root-form: הִתְקַטֵּל, "he killed himself". It is the reflexive of the intensive, and its meanings include acting on oneself, showing oneself as, reciprocal action, and acting for one's own benefit.

## The form

The prefix hith- marks the stem. Before a sibilant the t and the sibilant change places (metathesis): הִשְׁתַּמֵּר for the expected הִתְשַׁמֵּר. Before a dental the t is assimilated. These are the only points that can hide the prefix, so learn to see through them.

## The meanings

Hitpael is properly reflexive of Piel — "to sanctify oneself". But it very often means "to show oneself as" or "to give oneself out as" performing the action: "to show oneself revengeful", "to give oneself out to be rich". It may express reciprocal action ("they looked on one another") or action for oneself ("to walk about for oneself"). These shades share the idea of the subject turned back on itself.

## Niphal and Hitpael

The two are not the same. Niphal is the simple reflexive/passive of Qal; Hitpael is the reflexive of the intensive Piel. They differ in formation and in history, and they are never interchangeable. When you meet a reflexive idea, decide first whether the underlying verb is simple (Niphal) or intensive (Hitpael).

## Common confusion

Do not treat Hitpael as merely "passive". Its core is reflexivity — the subject acting on or for itself — and the "show oneself as" nuance is frequent. Read the prefix, recover the root, and let the reflexive force guide the translation.

## Worked example

Take הִתְפַּלֵּל ("he prayed"). The hith- prefix marks Hitpael, and the meaning is reflexive — literally "he judged himself", hence "he prayed". The subject acts on itself, which is the core idea to look for in every Hitpael.

## Reading practice

Read and parse. הִתְפַּלֵּל הָאִישׁ — "the man prayed" (Hitpael, reflexive). הִתְקַדֵּשׁ הָעָם — "the people sanctified themselves". The hith- prefix marks the stem, and the subject acting on itself is the meaning.
