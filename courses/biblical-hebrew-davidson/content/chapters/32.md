# Unit 32 — Questions and Negation

Turn a statement into a question, and deny it.

## Summary

A question is marked by the prefix הֲ or by a question word; the word order need not change. A statement is denied with לֹא, a command or wish with אַל, and existence with אֵין, the opposite of יֵשׁ.

## The interrogative prefix

The interrogative הֲ is an inseparable prefix that turns a statement into a question: הֲזֶה (is this?). Its pointing varies — usually הֲ, but before a simple shewa it is הֶ, and before gutturals the vowel adjusts. This single prefixed letter is the ordinary sign of a direct or indirect question.

Because the interrogative is attached, it can be easy to overlook at the beginning of a long pointed word. Check for it before parsing the noun or verb that follows. Its vowel is not perfectly uniform: the following consonant's shewa or guttural affects the pointing, so a changed vowel does not necessarily indicate a different interrogative particle. The syntax and context tell you whether the clause is a question rather than a statement.

## Negation

לֹא denies a statement: לֹא שָׁמַרְנוּ (we did not keep). אַל forbids or wishes against, with the jussive: אַל־תִּירָא (do not fear). אֵין (there is not) denies existence and negates a verbless sentence: אֵין מֶלֶךְ בְּיִשְׂרָאֵל (there was no king in Israel); its opposite is יֵשׁ (there is): יֵשׁ־עֵת לִשְׁמֹר (there is a time to keep).

## Common confusion

Do not take הֲ for the article. The article doubles the next consonant (הַמֶּלֶךְ); the interrogative usually has hatef patah and no doubling (הֲזֶה, is this?). And do not use לֹא for "do not" in a command: that is אַל with the jussive.
