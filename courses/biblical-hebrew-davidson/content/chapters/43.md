# Unit 43 — The Infinitives

Use the infinitive construct and the infinitive absolute.

## Summary

Hebrew has two infinitives. The infinitive construct is a verbal noun, usually with לְ (לִשְׁמֹר, to keep) or another preposition, and often with a suffix (בְּשָׁמְרוֹ, when he kept). The infinitive absolute stands alone, often before a finite verb of the same root to stress it: שָׁמוֹר תִּשְׁמְרוּ (you shall surely keep).

## The two forms

The infinitive construct is a noun made from the verb, and with לְ it means "to do": לִשְׁמֹר, "to keep". The infinitive absolute is a rarer emphatic form such as שָׁמוֹר.

## Two infinitives, two jobs

The infinitive construct behaves like a verbal noun. It can take a preposition—especially לְ—and pronominal suffixes, and it commonly follows verbs such as “begin,” “continue,” or “cease,” or expresses purpose (“in order to…”). The infinitive absolute is different: it stands without a prefix or suffix and often accompanies a finite verb to emphasize the action (“surely,” “indeed”) or to suggest continuation. Davidson stresses that these infinitives are of independent origin, not merely two versions of one form. When parsing, identify which one you have before translating its role.

## Common confusion

The infinitive construct with לְ usually translates "to …"; do not parse it as a finite verb. And an infinitive absolute before a verb of the same root adds emphasis ("surely"), not a second action.

## Source

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §21.
