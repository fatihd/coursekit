# Unit 44 — Transitive and Stative Verbs

Distinguish active, intransitive, and stative Qal verbs and their forms.

## Summary

The Qal perfect may end in any of three vowels: a, e, or o. Verbs in a are transitive (active); verbs in e and o are intransitive or stative — they describe the condition of the subject. The distinction shapes the whole verb: an active verb forms its imperfect in o, a stative verb in a, and the stative perfect is often best translated as an English present ("I know", "I am old").

## Active and stative

Verbs are named after the vowel of their perfect: a-verbs, e-verbs, and o-verbs. Verbs ending in a are transitive; verbs in e and o are intransitive, though the terms do not correspond exactly to those of Western languages. The intransitive class is wide, embracing words that describe the condition of the subject — to be full, to thirst, to fear, to love — even when they can take an object. These are called stative verbs, verbs of state, physical (to be great, to be old) or mental (to rejoice, to hate).

## The forms of the stative

The two classes differ throughout. The active verb (perfect in a) forms its imperfect in o: קָטַל gives יִקְטֹל. The stative verb (perfect in e or o) forms its imperfect in a: כָּבֵד (to be heavy) gives יִכְבַּד. The participle of the active verb is קֹטֵל; the stative's participle is the same as its perfect, a verbal adjective — זָקֵן (old), יָרֵא (fearing).

## The stative perfect in translation

The perfect of a stative verb usually corresponds to the English present: יָדַעְתִּי, "I know" (not "I knew"); זָקַנְתִּי, "I am old". This is found with other verbs of affection and mental state: "I trust", "I remember". The condition or state is regarded as the abiding result of a past experience, and Hebrew expresses that abiding state with the perfect.

## Common confusion

Do not translate every Qal perfect as a past tense. A stative perfect — "I know", "I am old", "I fear" — is a present state. And do not confuse stative with intransitive: not all intransitive verbs are stative (to reign is intransitive but not stative), and some stative verbs take an object (to love, to put on). Let the vowel and the meaning together decide.

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §22.
