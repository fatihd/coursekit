# Unit 22 — Prefix Prepositions

Recognize the prepositions that attach to the next word.

## Summary

Three common prepositions attach directly to the front of the word they govern: בְּ (in, with), לְ (to, for), and כְּ (like, as), and a fourth, מִ (from), does so in a shortened form. Because they are prefixes, they are read together with the noun, and their short vowel changes before an article or a guttural. Recognise the prefix and read it with its noun as one unit.

## Prefix prepositions


| Prefix | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| בְּ | in, with | בַּבַּיִת (in the house) |
| לְ | to, for | לַמֶּלֶךְ (to the king) |
| כְּ | like, as | כְּמֶלֶךְ (like a king) |
| מִ | from | מִבַּיִת (from a house) |


## The prefixed prepositions

בְּבַיִת is "in a house", לַמֶּלֶךְ "to the king", כְּמֶלֶךְ "like a king", מִבַּיִת "from a house". When the article follows, the two prefixes combine: בְּ + הַ becomes בַּ, לְ + הַ becomes לַ, and the article's doubling moves onto the noun. This combination is extremely common, so learn to spot it.

## Vowel changes

Before a noun with a simple shewa, בְּ/לְ/כְּ take a short î or e to avoid two shewas together. Before a guttural with a composite shewa, they take the vowel of that composite shewa. These adjustments are regular and predictable once you know the shewa rules.

## Common confusion

Do not separate the prefix from its noun when translating. בְּ is not a word standing alone; it is "in" or "with" attached to the next word. When you see בַּ, לַ, or כַּ with a doubled following consonant, read it as the preposition plus the definite article.

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §14.
