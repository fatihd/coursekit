# Unit 68 — Doubly Weak and Defective Verbs

Handle verbs with two weak letters and verbs that borrow forms.

## Summary

Some verbs have two weak letters and show both sets of changes; others are defective, lacking some forms, which are supplied by a related root. נָתַן (give) loses its first נ in the imperfect (יִתֵּן) and its last נ before a consonant ending (נָתַתִּי, I gave). הָלַךְ (go) forms its imperfect like a Pe-Yod verb: יֵלֵךְ.

## Doubly weak verbs

A verb may share the peculiarities of two weak classes — a Pe-Yod and Lamed-He verb, for example. Such a verb is named after all the classes whose features it shows, and each weak letter is treated by its own rule. Read the two weak letters in turn, and the form becomes regular.

## Defective verbs

Some verbs are defective: they lack one or more of the expected forms entirely. A particular stem or conjugation may simply not occur. Recognise the gap rather than forcing a form that does not exist. טוֹב (be good), for example, takes its imperfect and Hiphil from יָטַב: יִיטַב (it is good), הֵיטִיב (he did good).

## Common confusion

Do not stop at the first weak letter. In יִתֵּן the first נ of נתן has assimilated and the last remains; in נָתַתִּי the last נ has assimilated as well.

## Source

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §45.
