# Unit 45 — Nouns from Lamed-He Roots

Read nouns built on Lamed-He roots and common irregular nouns.

## Summary

Nouns from Lamed-He roots end in a vowel that is the trace of the original final he or yod: שָׂדֶה (field), מַחֲנֶה (camp). Alongside them stand a number of very common nouns whose inflection is irregular in detail — אָב (father), אָח (brother), בֵּן (son), בַּת (daughter), בַּיִת (house), יוֹם (day), מַיִם (water) — and these must be learned by observation, since they appear constantly.

## Lamed-He nouns

Nouns whose root ends in he or yod keep that weak letter as a final vowel: שָׂדֶה (field), מַחֲנֶה (camp), קֶצֶה (end). The final -eh is the third radical, not a case ending. Their inflection is regular once the weak ending is recognized, and they frequently overlap with Lamed-Aleph nouns in spelling.

## Common irregular nouns

A few very common nouns inflect irregularly and should be learned by observation: אָב (father), with construct אֲבִי (father of) and plural אָבוֹת (fathers); אָח (brother), בֵּן (son), בַּת (daughter); בַּיִת (house), plural בָּתִּים (houses); יוֹם (day), plural יָמִים (days); מַיִם (water), and פָּנִים (face). Their plurals and constructs are not what the ordinary patterns would predict. Compare each absolute form with its listed plural and construct; do not infer the paradigm from spelling alone.

## Common confusion

Do not force these common nouns into the regular patterns. The very words most used — father, brother, son, house, day, water — are the ones whose plurals and constructs depart from the rule. Learn them by frequent reading, and recognize that a "regular-looking" ending is no guarantee of a regular paradigm.

## Source

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §45.
