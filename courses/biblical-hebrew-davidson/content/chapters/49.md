# Unit 49 — Hiphil

Learn the Hiphil stem and its causative meanings.

## Summary

Hiphil is the causative stem. It prefixes he with an i vowel to the stem and lengthens the final vowel to i: קָטַל (he killed) becomes הִקְטִיל (he caused to kill). The imperfect is יַקְטִיל. Hiphil is extremely common, and its meaning — to make happen — is the backbone of many everyday verbs of bringing, causing, and making.

## The form

The perfect prefixes הִ- with an i (a thinned a) and closes with a long i; the imperfect prefixes יַ- and keeps the same final syllable. The jussive is the one place where Hiphil differs visibly from the imperfect — it shortens that final vowel. Recognise the he prefix and the characteristic i-vowel, and the stem is identified.

## The meanings

Hiphil is first of all causative of Qal: "to sanctify" is literally "to cause to be holy". It is also declaratory — הִצְדִּיק, "to declare someone righteous, to acquit", and הִרְשִׁיעַ, "to declare wicked, to condemn". And it is very often used of actions or states that English expresses by an intransitive: "to trust", "to be strong". The causative idea is genuinely present even where English does not show it.

## Common confusion

Do not translate every Hiphil with a forced "caused to". The causative, declaratory, and intransitive-looking uses are all real, so read the sense from context. But keep the stem's core in mind: the Hiphil extends the action outward, over a second agent.

## Worked example

Take הִגְדִּיל ("he magnified, he made great"). The he prefix with i, and the long final i, mark Hiphil, and the meaning is causative of גָּדַל ("to be great"). Read the he prefix first, then supply "caused to" or "made" as the sense.

## Reading practice

Read and parse. הִגְדִּיל יְהוָה — "the LORD magnified" (Hiphil, causative). הִשְׁמִיעַ אֶת־קוֹלוֹ — "he made his voice heard". The he prefix with the i-vowel is the mark of Hiphil; supply "caused to" or "made" as the sense.
