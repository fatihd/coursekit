# Unit 75 — Waw and Circumstantial Clauses

Tell narrative waw from the waw that describes circumstances.

## Summary

Waw links clauses. Before a verb it usually carries the story forward: וַיֹּאמֶר (and he said). Before a noun or pronoun it usually pauses the sequence to describe the circumstances or to set up a contrast: וְהוּא יֹשֵׁב (while he was sitting).

## Waw

The conjunction ו links nouns, phrases, and whole clauses. At the start of a clause it often carries the narrative along: וַיֹּאמֶר, "and he said". The vowel of ו adjusts to the next word, but its joining force is constant. Distinguish simple "and" from the narrative "and then" by context.

## Circumstantial clauses

A clause that opens with waw and a noun or pronoun, rather than a verb, usually gives the background: וְהַנָּחָשׁ הָיָה עָרוּם, "now the serpent was more cunning"; וְהוּא צֹלֵעַ עַל־יְרֵכוֹ, "while he was limping on his thigh". English shows this with "while", "now", "as", or "but".

## Common confusion

Do not read every waw as "and then". Waw before a verb moves the story on; waw before a noun or pronoun usually pauses to describe or to contrast.

## Source

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §15, §23.
