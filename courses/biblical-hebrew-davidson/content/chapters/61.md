# Unit 61 — Hollow Verbs (Ayin-Waw and Ayin-Yod)

Recognize weak middle radicals and their vowel changes.

## Summary

Hollow verbs have a waw or yod as the middle radical, and that letter acts as a vowel rather than a consonant. קָם (he arose, from קום) becomes יָקוּם (he will arise), and בָּא (he came, from בוא) becomes יָבוֹא. The long vowel in the middle of the word is the trace of the weak radical. Because the middle letter is a vowel, the infinitive construct is treated as the ground-form.

## The ground-form

In hollow verbs the infinitive construct, not the perfect, is the ground-form, because the perfect does not show the characteristic waw or yod. So קוּם (to arise) is the base, and the perfect קָם and imperfect יָקוּם are built from it. Learn the infinitive as the key form.

## The imperfect

The imperfect keeps the vowel of the ground-form and uses an old preformative ya lengthened to yā in the open syllable: יָקוּם, יָבוֹא. The jussive and the waw-consecutive imperfect are shortened (קָם, יָקָם). Only in the Qal imperfect do the waw and yod classes differ; in the Hiphil they fall together.

## Hiphil and Hophal

The Hiphil is regular in form — הֵקִים — with the preformative vowel lengthened in the open syllable; the Hophal is הוּקַם. Because the i and u vowels are so characteristic of these verbs, a helping vowel is often inserted between the stem and the consonantal endings to keep them in an open syllable.

## Common confusion

Hollow verbs are short because the middle radical is only a vowel. Do not treat them as irregular exceptions; they follow a regular pattern once the root is known to be hollow. The perfect's long ā (קָם) and the imperfect's long û (יָקוּם) mark the same weak middle letter.

## Worked example

Take קָם ("he arose", from קום) and יָקוּם ("he will arise"). The middle waw surfaces as a long vowel — ā in the perfect, û in the imperfect. The infinitive קוּם shows the weak letter most clearly, so it is the ground-form to learn.

## Reading practice

Read and parse. קָם הָאִישׁ — "the man arose". בָּא הָעָם — "the people came". The long vowel (ā, û, ô) in the middle is the weak radical; learn the infinitive (קוּם, בּוֹא) as the ground-form.

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §40.
