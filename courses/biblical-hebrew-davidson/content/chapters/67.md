# Unit 67 — Apocopated Forms

Recognize the shortened jussive and wayyiqtol of Lamed-He verbs.

## Summary

In Lamed-He verbs the jussive and the imperfect after waw consecutive drop their final ־ֶה: יִהְיֶה becomes יְהִי (let it be), and the narrative form is וַיְהִי (and it was). The shortened, or apocopated, form often needs a helping vowel: וַיִּבֶן (and he built), from יִבְנֶה.

## Apocopated forms

Apocopation drops a final letter or vowel, especially in the jussive and before waw. The shortened form is the point of contact with the jussive and the waw-consecutive, where an open final syllable is cut back. The cut-off form may end in two consonants, which Hebrew resolves with a helping vowel, as in the segholate nouns: יִבְנֶה (he will build) becomes וַיִּבֶן, יִגְלֶה becomes וַיִּגֶל. Sometimes no helping vowel is added: וַיֵּשְׁתְּ (and he drank), וַיַּרְא (and he saw).

## Worked example

A shortened jussive such as יְהִי ("let it be") drops its final vowel before waw or in the jussive. Recognise the short form by its missing ending, and recover the full form from context. Doubly weak verbs simply apply two weak-letter rules at once.

## Reading practice

Read and parse. וַיְהִי — "and it came to pass" (apocopated imperfect of היה with waw). The shortened form drops its final vowel; recover the full form from context and from the weak-letter rules.

## Common confusion

Do not look for a new root in a short form: וַיַּרְא is from רָאָה (see), וַיִּבֶן from בָּנָה (build). Restore the lost ־ֶה and the verb is familiar.
