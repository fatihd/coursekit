# Unit 28 — Segholate Nouns (Second Declension)

Inflect nouns like מֶלֶךְ and סֵפֶר.

## Summary

Segholates like מֶלֶךְ (king) and סֵפֶר (book) are stressed on their first syllable. They are really one syllable, malk and sipr, with a helping vowel between the last two consonants; before a suffix the original form returns: מַלְכִּי (my king). The plural takes new vowels: מְלָכִים (kings).

## The segholates

A segholate looks like a two-syllable word accented on the first syllable (מֶלֶךְ, king; סֵפֶר, book; קֹדֶשׁ, holiness). It is really a monosyllable — malk, siphr, qodhsh — into which a furtive vowel (seghol) has been slipped to separate the final two consonants, which Hebrew will not pronounce together. With an inflectional addition the word returns to its monosyllabic core: מַלְכִּי (my king), סִפְרוֹ (his book). The plural takes a helping a: מְלָכִים (kings), סְפָרִים (books).

## The forms

| Form | a-type | i-type | o-type |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| singular | מֶלֶךְ | סֵפֶר | קֹדֶשׁ |
| with suffix | מַלְכִּי | סִפְרִי | קָדְשִׁי |
| plural | מְלָכִים | סְפָרִים | קֳדָשִׁים |
| construct plural | מַלְכֵי | סִפְרֵי | קָדְשֵׁי |

## Common confusion

Do not stress a segholate on its last syllable: מֶלֶךְ is MEH-lekh. Before a suffix it is one syllable again (מַלְכִּי), and in the plural it takes the vowels of a strong noun (מְלָכִים).
