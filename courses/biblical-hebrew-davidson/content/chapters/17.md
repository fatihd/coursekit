# Unit 17 — Adjectives and the Verbless Sentence

Make adjectives agree, and read two-part sentences without a verb.

## Summary

An adjective that describes a noun follows it and agrees in gender, number, and definiteness: הַמֶּלֶךְ הַטּוֹב (the good king). An adjective that says something about a noun is a predicate: it agrees in gender and number but never takes the article, and it often comes first: טוֹב הַמֶּלֶךְ (the king is good). Hebrew needs no verb "to be" for such sentences.

## Definiteness and agreement


| Phrase | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| הַמֶּלֶךְ הַטּוֹב | the good king |
| מֶלֶךְ טוֹב | a good king |
| הַמַּלְכָּה הַטּוֹבָה | the good queen |

## Adjective agreement

An attributive adjective follows its noun and copies its gender and number: מֶלֶךְ טוֹב (a good king), מַלְכָּה טוֹבָה (a good queen). When the noun is definite, the adjective takes the article too: הַמֶּלֶךְ הַטּוֹב (the good king). This shared definiteness is a strong clue that an adjective modifies a noun rather than standing as a predicate.

## The verbless sentence

Hebrew needs no verb "to be" in the present. Two words can make a whole sentence: טוֹב הָאוֹר (the light is good), הָאִישׁ חָכָם (the man is wise). An adjective used as the predicate agrees with its noun in gender and number but does not take the article, even after a definite noun, and it often comes first: גָּדוֹל הַחֹשֶׁךְ (the darkness is great). A noun or a phrase with a preposition can be the predicate too: הֶעָפָר עַל־הַמַּיִם (the dust is upon the waters). A third-person pronoun sometimes stands between subject and predicate: הָהָר הוּא רָם (the mountain is high). English supplies "is" or "are"; "was" or "will be" comes from the context.

## Common confusion

Do not translate every adjective as a predicate. מֶלֶךְ טוֹב is "a good king", an attributive phrase; טוֹב הַמֶּלֶךְ (or with a change of order) can be "the king is good", a predicate. The word order and the article tell you which reading is meant.

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §11.
