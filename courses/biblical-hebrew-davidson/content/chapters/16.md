# Unit 16 — The Definite Article

Mark a noun as definite with the article.

## Summary

Hebrew marks a noun as definite by prefixing the article הַ- and doubling the next consonant: מֶלֶךְ (a king), הַמֶּלֶךְ (the king). There is no indefinite article; a bare noun is indefinite. Before a guttural or ר, which cannot be doubled, the article's vowel changes instead.

## The article

The article is the syllable הַ- prefixed to the noun, with the consonant after it doubled: מֶלֶךְ (a king) becomes הַמֶּלֶךְ (the king). Before a guttural the doubling is often dropped and the vowel adjusts. The article makes a specific, known thing; without it the noun is general or indefinite.

## Before gutturals

א, ע, and ר refuse the doubling, so the article lengthens to הָ: הָאִישׁ (the man), הָעִיר (the city), הָרֹאשׁ (the head). Before ה and ח the doubling is implied and the article stays הַ: הַהֵיכָל (the palace), הַחֹשֶׁךְ (the darkness). Before ח with qamets, and before an unstressed הָ or עָ, it becomes הֶ: הֶחָכָם (the wise one), הֶעָפָר (the dust); but הָהָר (the mountain), הָעָם (the people), where הָ and עָ are stressed.

## Common confusion

Do not count the doubled consonant after הַ as part of the root. הַמֶּלֶךְ is ha + melek; the dagesh in מ belongs to the article.

## Source

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §11.
