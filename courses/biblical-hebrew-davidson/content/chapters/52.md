# Unit 52 — Construct Chains and Genitive Relations

Read the construct state and its genitive relations.

## Summary

When one noun is bound closely to a following noun, the first stands in the construct state and the second, often definite, supplies the genitive: בֵּית הַמֶּלֶךְ (the house of the king, "the king's house"). The construct noun is usually shortened and never takes the article; only the last noun of the chain is made definite. Translating "X of Y" — and watching for long chains — is the skill here.

## Construct chains


| Phrase | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| בֵּית הַמֶּלֶךְ | the house of the king |
| דְּבַר הָאֱלֹהִים | the word of God |
| בֵּן הָאִישׁ | the son of the man |


## The construct state

The first noun in a construct chain drops its article and often shortens its vowels: בַּיִת (house) becomes בֵּית (house of) in בֵּית הַמֶּלֶךְ. The meaning is possession or close relation, exactly like English "of" or the apostrophe-s. Only the final noun carries the article, and its definiteness governs the whole chain.

## Reading a chain

A chain can be longer than two nouns: בֵּית אֲבִי הַמֶּלֶךְ, "the house of the father of the king". Each noun except the last is in the construct state. Read from the last definite noun backward, or translate each "of" in order, to keep the relationships straight.

## Common confusion

Do not translate the construct as if the first noun had the article. בֵּית הַמֶּלֶךְ is "the house of the king", not "the house the king". The article belongs to the final noun but makes the whole phrase definite. Watch for the shortened construct form, which can look like a different word.

Davidson, *An Introductory Hebrew Grammar* (22nd ed., 1923), §17.
